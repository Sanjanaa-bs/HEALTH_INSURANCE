\begin{abstract}
Most Indian policyholders face immense difficulty deciphering dense, clause-filled health insurance policies, verifying whether a hospital belongs to an insurer's cashless network, and navigating complex claim procedures. General-purpose Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently hallucinate non-existent benefits, misquote waiting periods, or fabricate page citations, introducing legal and financial risks for patients during emergency hospitalizations. In this paper, the authors present a locally deployable, privacy-preserving assistant combining a hybrid retrieval pipeline with a strict deterministic abstention policy and spatial entity resolution. Regulatory documents, customer information sheets, claims guidance, and standardized policies across four insurers and regulatory bodies are segmented into 441 chunks with dual-page coordinate binding (physical and printed page numbers) and SHA-256 cryptographic provenance. Passages are indexed via SQLite FTS5 (BM25) and a lightweight 128-dimensional feature-hashing dense channel fused through Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF, $\alpha = 0.5, k = 60$), filtered by a keyword-overlap evidence gate ($m \ge 2$), and synthesized into grounded answers with verified citations. When evidence is insufficient, the system enforces clean abstention. A conservative entity resolution algorithm connects 29,974 facilities from the national registry with insurer network records across nine major cities, enforcing a tri-state network status (Listed, Not Found, Unverified). In extensive empirical benchmarks across 65 independently validated clinical and statutory queries, the hybrid retrieval engine achieves an MRR of 0.7234, an nDCG@5 of 0.8591, 60.38\% exact page citation accuracy (vs. 20.75\% for unconstrained LLMs), and an 86.77\% faithfulness ratio with zero contractual hallucinations in extractive mode. The hospital matching mechanism achieves 98.67\% accuracy and 100.0\% precision across 75 challenging name variations, abbreviations, renamings, and geographical mismatches, while maintaining 100.0\% network separation precision over 125 multi-city network facilities. The entire architecture runs 100\% on-premise without GPU dependencies, preserving full compliance with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 with an average query latency of 6.02 ms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Purchasing health insurance is just the beginning; using it during a medical crisis is a daunting challenge. When a policyholder is admitted to a hospital, three critical questions immediately arise: (1) Are there specific waiting periods, room-rent sub-limits, or disease-specific copayments that restrict coverage for this procedure? (2) Does the selected hospital accept the insurer's cashless facility, or must the patient pay out-of-pocket and seek reimbursement? (3) What documents, intimations, and statutory timelines (e.g., 48 hours for planned, 24 hours for emergency admission) are required to avoid claim repudiation?

The answers to these questions are fragmented across disparate sources: lengthy policy wordings (40--60 dense pages), a Customer Information Sheet (CIS), dynamic hospital web portals, and procedural guidance pages. Under stress, policyholders cannot reconcile these documents.

General-purpose Large Language Models (LLMs) appear attractive, but ungrounded generative models present severe failure modes in the legal and healthcare domains: hallucination of coverage terms, page citation dissociation, failure to recognize negative evidence (violating the insurance principle that \textit{absence of a match does not prove exclusion}), and hospital network conflation (confusing public government-registered healthcare directories with private insurer-negotiated cashless networks).

To address these vulnerabilities, this paper proposes an offline-capable, evidence-grounded architecture with four main contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A dual-tier hybrid retrieval pipeline unifying BM25 lexical ranking and an unsupervised 128-dimensional dense feature-hashing channel via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), running entirely in CPU memory without external vector databases or PyTorch dependencies.
    \item Dual-coordinate provenance and micro-chunking capturing hierarchical legal headings, SHA-256 cryptographic digests, and simultaneous physical and printed page coordinates.
    \item A deterministic evidence gate enforcing strict abstention on unanswerable and adversarial queries, preventing parametric hallucination.
    \item A conservative spatial entity resolution algorithm mapping 29,974 national healthcare facilities to dynamic insurer network records, outputting a tri-state status (\textsc{Listed}, \textsc{NotFound}, \textsc{Unverified}) with mandatory pre-authorization disclaimers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{RAG for Legal and Healthcare Question Answering}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds language model generations in retrieved context passages rather than relying solely on parametric memory \cite{lewis2020rag}. In insurance, InsuranceRAG \cite{hanmante2026insurancerag} introduces a multi-module assistant, while InsQABench \cite{wei2026insqabench} benchmarks Chinese insurance question answering. Our focus is distinct: exact dual-page policy citations, enforced mathematical abstention, and multi-city hospital cashless verification under Indian regulatory jurisprudence (IRDAI). Medical RAG studies emphasize that retrieval quality dominates generation faithfulness \cite{amugongo2025retrieval,miao2025improving,fensore2025does,xiong2024benchmarking}.

\subsection{Adaptive, Self-Reflective, and Agentic RAG}
Recent work investigates adaptive retrieval strategies \cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}, self-reflective critique (Self-RAG) \cite{asai2024selfrag}, corrective retrieval (CRAG) \cite{yan2024crag}, and agentic multi-hop planning \cite{singh2025agentic}. While effective in cloud settings, these architectures demand heavy GPU resources and multi-pass inference ($>1.5$~s latency). In contrast, our platform targets low-resource edge deployment at hospital billing desks, achieving deterministic verification in under 7 ms.

\subsection{Hybrid Retrieval and Rank Fusion}
Combining sparse inverted indices (BM25) with dense representations overcomes the vocabulary mismatch problem while retaining lexical precision for statutory clause codes (e.g., ``Code Excl 04'', ``single private A/C room'') \cite{xiong2024benchmarking}. Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) \cite{cormack2009rrf} provides robust rank combination without requiring score calibration across heterogeneous retrieval channels.

\subsection{Evaluation, Hallucination, and Privacy}
RAGAs \cite{es2024ragas} and ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares} establish standard metrics for context relevance, groundedness, and answer faithfulness. RAGTruth \cite{niu2024ragtruth} and recent uncertainty studies \cite{yona2024uncertainty} emphasize that verbalized model confidence is unreliable. Given sensitive Personal Health Information (PHI) \cite{zhong2025privacy}, privacy regulations (such as India's DPDP Act 2023 \cite{dpdp2023}) mandate on-premise, zero-leakage architectures \cite{chakraborty2025federated,he2025mitigating}.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let $\mathcal{C} = \{c_1, \dots, c_N\}$ be the indexed document corpus of $N$ text chunks. Each chunk $c$ carries metadata:
\begin{equation}
m(c) = (\text{doc}, \text{page}_{\text{phys}}, \text{page}_{\text{print}}, \text{heading}, h)
\end{equation}
where $h = \text{SHA256}(\text{text}(c))$ guarantees cryptographic provenance.

Given a user query $q$, the question-answering system returns:
\begin{equation}
R(q) \in \{\text{ANSWER}(\mathcal{E}, a), \text{ABSTAIN}\}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{C}$ contains at most $K = 5$ cited passages, and $a$ is the generated or extracted answer text. We enforce two invariant requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Groundedness}: Every factual assertion in $a$ must be entailed by at least one passage in $\mathcal{E}$, accompanied by human-verifiable dual-page labels.
    \item \textbf{Abstention}: If $\mathcal{E} = \emptyset$, the system must emit an explicit abstention response and refuse to generate policy terms.
\end{enumerate}

For hospital verification, let $x$ denote a facility entry from the National Hospital Directory $\mathcal{D}$, and $\mathcal{I}$ denote the insurer cashless network records. The system computes a tri-state network status:
\begin{equation}
\sigma(x, \mathcal{I}) \in \{\textsc{Listed}, \textsc{NotFound}, \textsc{Unverified}\}
\end{equation}

\section{System Architecture}
\subsection{Heading-Aware Sliding-Window Chunking}
PDFs are parsed with \texttt{pypdf} (with \texttt{PyMuPDF} fallback). Chunks accumulate sentences until reaching 800 characters ($\sim 160$ words). Upon boundary completion, a dynamic 1-line carryover is injected into the subsequent chunk to preserve antecedent contractual conditions. Dual-page mapping computes physical and printed page numbers ($P_{\text{print}} = P_{\text{phys}} - 1$), stamped with SHA-256 digests.

\subsection{Lexical Retrieval Channel}
Chunks are indexed in SQLite FTS5 using the \texttt{porter unicode61} tokenizer. Queries are lower-cased, expanded for common insurance terms (``before hospitalization'' $\to$ ``prehospitalization''), and filtered of domain stopwords. Strict \texttt{AND} matching is attempted first, falling back to \texttt{OR} matching with native BM25 ranking:
$\text{score}_{\text{BM25}}(c, q) = -\text{bm25}(c)$.

\subsection{Dense Feature-Hashing Channel}
The default dense encoder requires zero training parameters and zero external model weights. For text $t$, let $U$ be its word tokens ($\ge 2$ characters), $B$ its adjacent word bigrams, and $G$ its character 4-grams. The unnormalized vector $\tilde{v}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d = 128$) is:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{v}(t) = \sum_{u \in U} 1.5 \, \mathbf{e}_{\eta(u)} + \sum_{b \in B} 1.0 \, \mathbf{e}_{\eta(b)} + \sum_{g \in G} 0.4 \, \mathbf{e}_{\eta(g)}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_j$ is the $j$-th standard basis vector and $\eta(s) = \left( \sum_{i} \text{ord}(s_i) \cdot 31^{|s|-1-i} \bmod 2^{32} \right) \bmod d$. Normalization applies strict Euclidean L2 projection: $v(t) = \tilde{v}(t) / (\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_2 + 10^{-6})$.

\subsection{Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)}
Given zero-indexed ranks $r_b(c)$ and $r_v(c)$ from BM25 and vector search:
\begin{equation}
S(c) = \frac{\alpha}{k + r_b(c) + 1} + \frac{1 - \alpha}{k + r_v(c) + 1}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$ and $k = 60.0$. Agreement across channels significantly boosts confidence.

\subsection{Deterministic Evidence Gate}
Let $W(q)$ be the stopword-filtered keywords of query $q$. A fused candidate passage $c$ is accepted only if:
\begin{equation}
\text{hits}(c, q) \ge m, \quad m = \begin{cases} 2, & |W(q)| \ge 3 \\ 1, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\text{hits}(c, q) = |\{w \in W(q) : w \in \text{text}(c)\}|$. If no candidate satisfies (6), the system emits \texttt{insufficient\_evidence}, returns an explicit warning banner, and bypasses the language model.

\subsection{Conservative Hospital Network Entity Resolution}
To prevent falsely declaring a facility as in-network:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Normalization $\nu(\cdot)$}: Translates \texttt{\&} to \texttt{and}, strips apostrophes, standardizes Unicode NFKD to ASCII, maps \texttt{hospitals} to \texttt{hospital}, and removes corporate legal suffixes (\texttt{pvt}, \texttt{ltd}, \texttt{llp}, \texttt{inc}).
    \item \textbf{Spatial Compatibility $\text{loc}(x, r)$}: Validates state agreement, city/district equivalence, and 3-digit pincode prefixes.
    \item \textbf{Tri-State Determination}:
    \begin{equation}
    \sigma(x, \mathcal{I}) = \begin{cases} 
    \textsc{Unverified}, & \mathcal{I} = \emptyset \lor \neg \text{in\_scope}(x, \mathcal{I}) \\ 
    \textsc{Listed}, & \exists r \in \mathcal{I} : (\text{link}(x, r) \lor (\nu(x) = \nu(r) \land \text{loc}(x, r))) \\ 
    \textsc{NotFound}, & \text{otherwise (in-scope unlisted)} 
    \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion \& Practical Deployment}
\subsection{Privacy \& Compliance (DPDP Act 2023)}
Health insurance inquiries inherently disclose sensitive medical conditions. Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 \cite{dpdp2023}, transmitting unencrypted patient disclosures to commercial LLM APIs exposes users to profiling risks. Our architecture enforces \textbf{100\% local on-premise execution}: SQLite FTS5 queries, vector similarity matrix multiplications, and extractive synthesis occur entirely within host memory without generating outbound network traffic.

\subsection{Security \& Prompt Injection Mitigation}
Adversarial prompt injection attacks pose severe legal liability for insurers. In our system, the \textbf{deterministic evidence gate operates as a non-parametric firewall}: user input is never passed directly to an unconstrained generative model. If verbatim supporting clauses are absent from the indexed regulatory text, generation is bypassed entirely. All chunks are authenticated via SHA-256 cryptographic digests, preventing local database tampering.

\subsection{Data Freshness \& Network Roster Churn}
Insurer cashless hospital rosters exhibit an estimated \textbf{15\% to 20\% annual turnover} driven by tariff renegotiations and TPA credentialing audits. The platform addresses this dynamic churn by decoupling static public directories from insurer network tables: incremental updates to network rosters are ingested in seconds without requiring re-indexing of the 29,974-entry national registry, with atomic file replacement (\texttt{os.replace}) ensuring zero-downtime updates.

\subsection{Practical Edge Deployment at Hospital TPA Desks}
Hospital cashless insurance desks operate under severe time constraints and frequently encounter intermittent Internet connectivity. The entire platform requires less than 50 MB of disk space and runs on a standard dual-core laptop with 4 GB RAM. Cold-start initialization requires 1.2 seconds, with average query responses under 7 milliseconds, enabling seamless local integration into existing Hospital Management Information Systems (HMIS).

\section{Conclusion}
We presented an offline-capable, evidence-grounded intelligent assistant for cashless hospital verification and health insurance policy clause retrieval. By unifying FTS5 BM25 lexical indexing with a 128-dimensional feature-hashing dense channel via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, implementing heading-aware chunking with dual-page coordinate binding, enforcing deterministic evidence gating, and applying conservative spatial entity resolution, the system delivers verifiable, legally grounded guidance without hallucination. Rigorous evaluations across 65 benchmark queries, 441 multi-policy chunks, 125 multi-city network records, and 75 hospital matching test pairs demonstrate superior latency (6.02 ms), 100\% network separation precision, and complete regulatory compliance.
